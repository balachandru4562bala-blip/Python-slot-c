--- Conceptual Inquiry & Explanations ---

--- Comparative Multi-Scale Performance Table ---
Data Size    | += Time (s)     | join() Time (s)  | StringIO Time (s)  | Speedup   
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
1000         | 0.000390        | 0.000020         | 0.000188           | 19.70x    
10000        | 0.003518        | 0.000426         | 0.001947           | 8.26x     
200000       | 0.444365        | 0.005652         | 0.031859           | 78.62x    

--- Technical Explanation ---
1. Why += can become O(n^2): Python strings are immutable.
   In the naive model, `text += p` creates a new buffer and copies all previous characters.
   Total characters copied = 1 + 2 + ... + n = O(n^2).
2. CPython Optimization: When reference count of `text` is 1 and no other variable aliases it,
   CPython's PyUnicode_Append reallocates the buffer in-place using amortized doubling, reducing += to O(n).
3. Why join() is ALWAYS superior: `str.join()` calculates the exact final buffer length in ONE pass,
   allocates memory ONCE, and performs memory-copy in C. It is guaranteed O(n) across all Python runtimes.
